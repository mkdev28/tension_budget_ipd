What is being measured, and why

The upper trapezius muscle is the primary site of work-related neck and shoulder pain — the most prevalent musculoskeletal disorder in office workers globally (prevalence 15–35%, Hoy et al. 2010). Unlike wrist or back injuries that are often acute, trapezius disorders develop through cumulative low-level activation that never fully relaxes, overloading a specific population of slow-twitch (Type I) motor units — the “Cinderella hypothesis” (Hägg 1991). Standard clinical observation cannot detect this mechanism; continuous sEMG quantification is required.

What the dataset enables

The 38-column feature vector logged every 5 minutes, paired with concurrent Borg CR-10 subjective strain ratings, creates a supervised longitudinal time-series dataset for:

Bayesian hierarchical regression of subjective muscular strain from physiological biomarkers

Fatigue onset prediction (from spectral MDF decline)

Subject-specific ergonomic threshold personalisation

Real-time intervention timing (when to alert, when to recommend breaks)

5.3 Exposure Index — Amplitude-Domain Load (per side)

These four columns implement the Koch et al. (2024) Exposure Index (EIndex) formula, tracking what fraction of each 5-minute epoch was spent in rest, low, and high muscular activation zones.

EI  =  −2 × Prest  +  Plow  +  2 × Phigh

P(rest), P(low), P(high) = proportion of RMS samples below 0.5%, between 0.5–7.0%, and above 7.0% MVE

Column

Type

Range

Description

eindex_live_left

REAL

[−2.0, +2.0]

Left trapezius EIndex computed on the current sliding 10-minute RMS window, refreshed every 2 s. Drives the real-time composite score. −2.0 = fully resting; +2.0 = high-load throughout. Never NULL.

eindex_live_right

REAL

[−2.0, +2.0]

Same metric for the right trapezius. The two sides are processed with zero cross-channel mixing. Never NULL.

eindex_cumulative_left

REAL

unbounded (monotonic)

Session-level cumulative EIndex for the left trapezius, incremented once per completed discrete 10-minute boundary (never on overlapping windows). Used for STAMI population-reference comparison only — never drives the real-time composite.

eindex_cumulative_right

REAL

unbounded (monotonic)

Same for the right trapezius.

Why two EIndex variants?

A single summed EIndex on a sliding window would double-count: a moderate 10-minute activity period would be counted hundreds of times across overlapping 2-second refresh cycles, diverging to ~5,760 over an 8-hour session. The live/cumulative split ensures each moment of activity is counted exactly once per metric.

gaps/min

Micro-rest frequency for the left trapezius. A gap is any period where %RVE < 3.0% lasting at least 0.125 s — complete muscular relaxation. The primary physiological recovery indicator. In occupational ergonomics, values below ~4 gaps/minute are associated with elevated shoulder-pain risk (Veiersted 1994). Never NULL.

Biological significance

The Cinderella motor units (lowest-threshold, first recruited) are only de-loaded when %RVE drops below the 3% gap threshold. Without adequate micro-rests, these units fire continuously throughout the workday, accumulating metabolic waste and leading to chronic myalgia even at sub-threshold force levels.

5.9 Spectral Fatigue Indicators (per side)

Quantify muscle fatigue via the frequency-domain signature of the sEMG signal. As trapezius fatigue develops, action-potential conduction velocity decreases and motor-unit firing rates slow, shifting spectral power toward lower frequencies. Median Power Frequency (MDF) is the gold-standard spectral fatigue indicator (De Luca 1997; Farina 2002).

Critical caveat — embedded in every output record

All spectral analysis uses the 20–240 Hz passband (hardware-constrained at 500 Hz sampling). The published De Luca/Farina standard uses 20–450 Hz (requires ≥ 1000 Hz sampling). Absolute MDF/MNF values are therefore systematically lower than published population norms. All spectral features must be interpreted as internal session-trend indicators only, never compared against published absolute baselines.

Median & Mean Power Frequency

∫20MDF S(f) df   =   ∫MDF240 S(f) df

MDF splits the Welch power spectral density into two equal-power halves

MNF  =  Σj fj × S(fj)   ÷   Σj S(fj)

MNF — power-weighted spectral centroid; more sensitive to high-frequency content and noise than MDF

Hz  [20–240]

Median Power Frequency. Computed from the pre-rectification bandpass-filtered signal using Welch's method (1 s windows, 50% overlap, Hanning taper). A downward trend across epochs signals progressive fatigue. Typical unfatigued values: ~80–120 Hz (passband-constrained).

Whether the subject submitted a Borg CR-10 rating at this epoch boundary. 1 = rating received; 0 = subject did not respond. Primary mechanism for handling missing self-reports — always written, even when no rating exists.

subjective_strain_cr10

REAL

[0.0, 10.0] or NULL

NULL when strain_reported = 0

Ground-truth subjective muscular strain label (Borg CR-10, collected via non-blocking popup every 5-minute epoch boundary). 0 = nothing at all; 5 = strong (heavy working fatigue); 10 = absolute maximum. This is the regression target y.

Missingness policy for the strain label (non-negotiable design requirement)

Missing ratings are represented as NULL / empty string — never as a sentinel number such as −1 or 0. This is required because:

1. Bayesian priors: sentinel values contaminate continuous prior distributions. A prior that treats y = −1 as a valid observation distorts posterior estimates by injecting a physically impossible data point.

2. Forward-fill (LOCF) is prohibited: carrying the last observation forward would inflate the effective sample size by 150,000× at the raw 500 Hz sample level — pseudo-replication that destroys confidence intervals downstream.

3. Backward-fill is prohibited: if the session terminates unexpectedly (hardware disconnect, battery failure), backward-filling from a future observation that was never recorded is impossible.

4. Imputed zero is prohibited: a rating of 0.0 (“no exertion at all”) has a specific valid physiological meaning and must not be substituted for “no rating received.”

8. Data Collection Protocol

Session setup (per subject, per recording)

1. Subject registration: subject enters name, birth date, sex, weight, and height into the GUI profile dialog. Creates a user_profiles row and a local user_profile.json.

2. Hardware connection: two Arduino UNO R4 Minima identified via Device Manager (COM port numbers entered into start_lsl_stream.py). LSL stream started; GUI confirms stream discovery.

3. Electrode placement: two Ag/AgCl surface electrodes per side placed on the upper trapezius belly (between C7 spinous process and acromion), with a reference electrode on a bony landmark (e.g. clavicle). Skin-electrode impedance is verified visually via the signal-quality indicator.

4. 5-second RVE calibration: subject performs a maximal bilateral shoulder shrug for 5 seconds. Mean RMS during this period is stored as calibration_rms_left and calibration_rms_right. All subsequent %RVE values are normalised to these baselines.

5. Task performance: subject performs their normal computer work task (typing, mousing, reading, etc.) under naturalistic conditions, with no imposed movement protocols.

6. Every 5 minutes: a Borg CR-10 popup appears automatically. Subject enters perceived exertion on [0.0, 10.0] and clicks Submit. Skipping the dialog sets strain_reported = 0, subjective_strain_cr10 = NULL.

7. Session end: subject clicks Stop Recording. end_session() writes CSVs/Parquet to disk first, then triggers asynchronous background cloud sync over HTTPS/Port 443.

Recording environment constraints

EMG signal processing starts only after the calibration shrug completes

Sessions with failed calibration (calibration_rms_left = NULL or 0) are flagged uncalibrated

Mains interference (50 Hz spike) is auto-detected via PSD comparison and a notch filter is applied automatically
